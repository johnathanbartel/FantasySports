BACKGROUND (decisions the owner has settled; do not reopen them, and write them into docs/ncaab_spec.md)
1. Rosters: 10 players, 5 starters (PG, SG, SF, PF, C) and 5 bench. This matches `ncaab_reference.py`, where a starter slot accepts the position plus G (PG, SG) or F (SF, PF).
2. One WINDOW is one fantasy game: one head-to-head result between two fantasy teams. There are no matchup periods and no fixed weekly shape for NCAAB. A season is a sequence of windows covering the regular season. (Hockey, by contrast, has three fixed windows per week; see `series_schedule.py`, `window_sports.py` and `docs/hockey_spec.md` section 5. Reuse their ideas, but NCAAB windows are variable length and come from the optimizer.)
3. Fantasy time is US Eastern. A "day" starts at 4am Eastern (so a late West Coast tip stays with the previous game day). The machine has no tz database installed, so convert UTC to Eastern with hand-coded daylight-saving rules: DST starts the second Sunday in March at 2am EST (07:00 UTC) and ends the first Sunday in November at 2am EDT (06:00 UTC).
4. The number of windows is NOT fixed. Each season the program computes the best boundaries for a range of window counts and reports the curve; the season or the users pick a point. Do not hard-code 24, 28 or 31.
5. Playoffs, conference tournaments and championships are set up by each league. The platform provides only the regular-season windows. The regular-season span ends at the Monday 4am after the last full Monday-to-Sunday week before the first conference-tournament game: 2023-24 ends Mon 2024-03-04, 2024-25 ends Mon 2025-02-24, 2025-26 ends Mon 2026-03-02. Each season starts on its first Monday 4am: 2023-11-06, 2024-11-04, 2025-11-03.
6. Flexing (spec only in this ticket): if a player's team plays several games inside one window, only one counts for that window (as in hockey, at most one game score per player per window). One of the extra games may be moved to a LATER window, to any later window that season (hockey only allows an open window later that same week). The moved game can be placed in any position the player is currently eligible for, and the manager can either start him or set him as the backup. A game moves forward only, never to a window that has already finished.
7. Everything not listed above is NOT decided; see OPEN QUESTIONS.

WHAT THE DATA SHOWED (analysis/ncaab_schedule/summary.md and the prototypes; numbers below are your acceptance targets, with the tolerance given in HOW TO VERIFY)
- Data: CollegeBasketballData.com, seasons 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26 (CBBD seasons 2024, 2025, 2026). 362, 364 and 365 Division I teams; 6,243 / 6,292 / 6,300 completed games (including games against non-D1 opponents). Teams play about 31 regular-season games (range 27-32). About 29% of teams play on a given day; Saturday holds about 43% of team-games, Thursday about 18%.
- The cost of a window (the number to minimize): for a 10-player roster with two players per position on different teams, a starter slot is empty in a window when neither of its two players' teams has a game in it. With p = the share of Division I teams that play at least once in the window, the expected number of empty starter slots in that window is 5 * (1 - p)^2. A season's cost is the sum over its windows. This is the "expected empty slot-windows per roster per season".
- The counterweight (reported, NOT minimized): surplus games. A surplus game is a game beyond a team's first inside one window. With s = the average over Division I teams of max(0, games in the window - 1), the expected surplus games for a 10-player roster (every player on a different team) in that window is 10 * s. A season's surplus is the sum over its windows: "expected surplus games per roster per season".
- The prototype optimizer was a dynamic program over a time grid (4-hour steps from the season start), minimum window length 24 hours, maximum 336 hours (14 days), choosing the cut points that minimize the summed cost for exactly N windows. With those settings the prototype found (expected empty slot-windows per roster per season, 4-hour grid):
    2023-24: N=24 0.5, N=28 1.2, N=31 2.8, N=34 7.0
    2024-25: N=24 0.6, N=28 1.8, N=31 5.2, N=34 10.6
    2025-26: N=24 0.4, N=28 0.9, N=31 2.5, N=34 6.3
  On a day grid (4am boundaries only) the same runs gave 0.6/1.3/3.0/7.1, 0.6/1.8/5.3/11.0 and 0.4/1.0/2.6/6.4. For comparison, a fixed weekly shape (Monday-to-Friday and Friday-to-Sunday windows plus a long holiday window) at 30-32 windows leaves 5.3-6.4.
- Fewer windows means fewer empty slots but more surplus games: at N=24 about 41-56 surplus games per roster per season, at N=28 24-33, at N=31 17-21. These came from a roster simulation, not the formula above, so treat them as a sanity range, not a pass/fail target.
- Windows land where the schedule is thin: Thanksgiving week, the second week of December, the days around Christmas, and the Nov 10-Dec 21 stretch. In conference season the optimizer rediscovers a Friday cut on its own (about 4-day then 2-3-day windows).
- Flexing a surplus game forward to any later window cut the 10-player empties at N=31 from 5.1 to 1.5 (same slot only) or 1.2 (guards share PG/SG and forwards share SF/PF) in the worst season; at N=28 it leaves 0.04-0.16.
- Assumptions to carry into the spec: the simulation puts every roster player on a different team and has no real player or position data; real clustering (stacking one team or conference) makes things worse; the windows come from the final schedule, so postponements and added games shift them.

STEPS
1. `ncaab_windows.py`: pure module, deterministic, standard library only. Suggested public names (adjust if the repo's style asks for something else):
   - `to_eastern(utc_naive) -> naive Eastern datetime` with the hand-coded DST rules above (assert it on the March and November 2024/2025 transition instants).
   - `GameRow(team_id, start_utc)` and `build_grid(start_et, end_et, step_hours=4)` (grid points counted from start_et, which is a Monday 4am Eastern).
   - `coverage_table(rows, team_ids, grid, max_hours=336)`: for every pair (a, b) of grid points with grid[b] - grid[a] <= max_hours, the share of `team_ids` with at least one game in [grid[a], grid[b]), the average team-games per team in it, and the average surplus per team (average of max(0, games - 1)). Pairs longer than max_hours are never needed; do not compute them. Compute it incrementally (sweep b upward for each a); do not use numpy.
   - `optimize_windows(coverage, n_windows, min_hours=24, max_hours=336, step_hours=4) -> WindowPlan(boundaries, cost, surplus, per_window)`: dynamic program minimizing the summed 5*(1-p)^2. Tie-break, exactly: among plans with equal cost (compare costs after rounding to 9 decimal places), choose the one whose first interior boundary is earliest; if those tie, the one whose second boundary is earliest, and so on (the lexicographically smallest boundary list). The same input must always yield the same plan.
   - `window_curve(rows, team_ids, start_et, end_et, counts, ...) -> list[CurvePoint]`: one point per window count with expected empty slot-windows, empty share, expected surplus games per roster per season, worst window coverage, number of windows with coverage >= 90%, window length (min / median / max in days) and team-games per window (min / max / standard deviation). Return plain dataclasses that serialize to JSON.
   Window boundaries are instants in Eastern time; a window runs from one boundary up to (not including) the next, and a game belongs to the window that contains its start. State this in the docstring.
